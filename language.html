Language

Euskara

Euskara, or Basque, is the traditional language of the Basque people. It is spoken in parts of Spain and France and is one of the oldest living languages in Europe. Today, Euskara has official status in much of the Spanish Basque Autonomous Community, where it is taught in schools and used in public life.

Language revitalization efforts during the late twentieth century significantly increased the number of speakers after periods when public use of Euskara had been restricted.

Origins and Linguistic Isolation

One of the most remarkable features of Euskara is that it is a language isolate. This means linguists have not been able to demonstrate a genetic relationship between Euskara and any other living language family.

Many theories have been proposed regarding its origins, but none have gained broad scholarly acceptance. Because of this, Euskara remains a unique survivor of Europe's pre-Indo-European linguistic landscape. While languages related to Latin, Germanic, or Slavic families spread across the continent, Euskara persisted in the western Pyrenees.

What Does Euskara Sound Like?

For many listeners, Euskara sounds noticeably different from neighboring Spanish and French. It contains sounds familiar to other European languages but follows distinct grammatical patterns.

Key features include:

  • A strong use of "s," "x," and "z" sounds.
  • Agglutinative grammar, in which suffixes are added to words to indicate meaning and grammar.
  • A highly regular spelling system, where words are generally pronounced as written.

Common phrases include:

  • Kaixo - Hello
  • Eskerrik asko - Thank you
  • Agur - Goodbye
