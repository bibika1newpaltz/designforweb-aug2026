Culture

Food

Basque cuisine reflects the region's Atlantic geography, combining coastal seafood traditions with agricultural products from inland valleys.

Notable dishes include:

  • Bacalao al Pil-Pil (salt cod)
  • Marmitako (tuna stew)
  • Txangurro (spider crab)
  • Gâteau Basque / Pastel Vasco
  • Pintxos, small dishes typically served in bars

Food also plays an important social role. Informal gatherings centered around shared meals have long been a key feature of community life.

Sports

Traditional Basque sports emerged from rural occupations and everyday labor.

Examples include:

Pelota

A fast-paced ball game played against a wall using the hand, racket, or basket.

Herri Kirolak (Rural Sports)

Traditional events developed from agricultural work, including:

  • Stone lifting (harrijasotzea)
  • Wood chopping (aizkolaritza)
  • Hay bale lifting
  • Tug-of-war competitions

These sports continue to be performed during festivals and local celebrations.

Folklore and Mythology

Basque mythology contains a rich collection of stories connected to mountains, caves, forests, and natural forces.

One of the most important figures is Mari, often described as a powerful supernatural being associated with weather, fertility, and the natural world. According to tradition, Mari resides in mountain caves and moves between different peaks.

Other well-known mythological beings include:

  • Basajaun ("Lord of the Forest"), a guardian spirit linked to wilderness and livestock.
  • Lamia, supernatural beings associated with rivers and streams.
  • Aatxe, a protective bull-like spirit appearing in folklore.

These traditions reveal how earlier Basque communities understood their relationship with landscape, nature, and the unseen world.
